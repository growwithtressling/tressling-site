Our Approach

Running the gauntlet

Transforming a business involves decisions about customers, operations, technology, and money. Our six-step process connects those decisions, from recognizing the need to change to realizing the value of a proven business.

We establish where your business is in the process and agree on the work required. Evidence from implementation informs the strategy as it develops.

1. Recognize the need to transform

Start with the reason customers buy from you. Examine how their needs and alternatives are changing, where AI can influence those choices, and which of your capabilities will remain valuable.

We assess the current business, the opportunity ahead, and the implications of timing. This creates a shared view of what needs to change and where to investigate further.

What this produces

A clear statement of the opportunity, the business assumptions to test, and the priorities for developing the strategy.

2. Build a financeable strategy

Choose where the business will compete, how it will create value, and what must be in place to deliver. The operating plan and financial model make the assumptions explicit and connect the proposed changes to their funding requirements.

We develop the strategy through four connected lenses:

People

Align leadership on the direction. Decide who leads each change and which roles change. Involve the people closest to the work, assess capabilities and capacity, and identify the training each role needs.

Processes

Map the actual workflows, decisions, handoffs, and exceptions. Design how the business will deliver its offer, with clear ownership and measures of performance.

Technology

Assess existing systems, data, and integrations against the business requirements. Determine what to retain, improve, connect, or build. Include security, reliability, user experience, and the ability of the team to operate and maintain the solution.

Capital

Estimate the cost and timing of the transition, including the resources required to keep the current business performing. Determine the cash it can contribute, identify additional funding needs, and evaluate financing options and milestones.

What this produces

An integrated operating and financial plan, with responsibilities, investment requirements, milestones, and assumptions that can be tested.

3. Execute

Translate the plan into manageable initiatives with agreed responsibilities and timelines. Work may include redesigning processes, arranging financing, integrating systems, developing an AI capability, or launching a new offer.

Your team participates in shaping and implementing the changes. Regular communication and practical support build confidence as the new approach becomes part of daily work.

What this produces

Changes in operation, clear ownership, and a regular review of progress and issues requiring decisions.

4. Prove it works

Compare results with the assumptions in the plan. Examine customer demand, adoption, delivery quality, margins, and cash flow. Agree on what the evidence supports and where adjustments are needed.

What this produces

A documented view of performance and the basis for the next investment decision.

5. Scale

Expand the offer and operating model that have demonstrated value. Match demand with the people, processes, technology, and capital required to deliver consistently. Continue reviewing performance as volume and complexity grow.

What this produces

An expansion plan connecting commercial demand, operating capacity, and funding.

6. Realize the value

Value is created in the first five steps. It is realized when the owner can take it out on terms that hold. That can be a sale, a succession to family or management, a recapitalization, or a business that pays its owner well and runs without depending on them. The owner chooses the form and the timing.

Preparation starts in the earlier steps: reliable financial statements, governance that holds through changes in leadership, a team that runs the business, and an owner who is ready to step back. When the time comes, we prepare the business and the owner, and work with buyers, lenders, and advisors through to closing.

What this produces

A business ready for the outcome the owner has chosen, and a completed sale, succession, or recapitalization on terms the owner accepts.
